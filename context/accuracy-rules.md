Every skill and command in this plugin that touches resume/cover-letter/outreach content
must follow this file exactly. It is not optional guidance — it's the standing constraint
that keeps every generated document something you could defend, unprepared, in an interview.

Candidate facts (roles, dates, certs), pivot targets, scoring guardrails, and the salary
floor live in `candidate-profile.md` — the single source of truth; the live master resume
(recipe in `config.md`) wins on any factual conflict.

---

## Overarching rule: No lies, no over-exaggeration

Every bullet point and competency claim must be something you could speak to confidently in
an interview without preparation. When in doubt, scope it down rather than up: "contributed
to" is safer than "led"; "supported" is safer than "owned."

## Scope/ownership framing (example — replace with your own)

If your real title understates or overstates your actual scope (e.g. you drove strategy for
a product you didn't own the roadmap for, or you were one of several people with the same
title), write down the precise, defensible way to describe it here — and the specific phrases
that would overclaim it. Example shape:

**Use language like:**
- "dedicated PM for adoption strategy for Product X"
- "one of three PMs focused on Y"

**Never use (without an explicit qualifier):**
- "sole PM for [platform]" if you weren't
- "built product vision and strategy from the ground up" if you didn't own the roadmap

## Hyperbole blacklist (example — replace with your own)

Keep a running list of phrases a resume-tailoring pass tends to reach for that you can't
actually back up. Example shape:
- "raised the enterprise bar for X" — replace with "informed," "supported," "contributed to"
- "guided investment decisions at global scale" — same

## Domain-adjacency claims (example — replace with your own)

If you have adjacent-but-not-practitioner knowledge in some domain (security, data science,
whatever), name the specific tools/systems where this applies and the language to use instead
of a stronger claim, so a tailoring pass doesn't accidentally claim practitioner-level
expertise you'd have to walk back in an interview.

## Job title accuracy

List any employer where the exact legal job title matters and shouldn't be informally
retitled (e.g. "Solutions Architect" should never become "Pre-Sales Solutions Architect" even
if the work was pre-sales-flavored) — describe the nuance in bullet points instead of the
title itself.

## Company name / tenure history quirks (example — replace with your own)

If an employer changed its legal name during your tenure, or your resume currently splits one
continuous job into two header entries (or vice versa) for a specific reason, write down the
canonical way to describe it so tailoring passes don't "fix" it inconsistently.

## Resume date formatting

Single-role companies: dates on the company line only, never duplicated on the title line.
Multi-role companies: each title line gets its own sub-role date range.

## Resume Baseline (MANDATORY)

**Always start tailored resumes from the master file** (path, live-read recipe, and
naming/save conventions in `config.md`). Never start from a previously tailored version —
even if the target role seems close; tailored versions accumulate narrative drift.

The salary floor and how to handle below-floor postings are defined in
`candidate-profile.md`.
